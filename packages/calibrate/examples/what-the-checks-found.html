What the checks found

We pointed the safety net at the real thing for the first time: paperweight's real engine, a real AI, and 11,521 papers' worth of real human reviews from another field. Here is what came back, in six short scenes.

    Real, but not yet the verdict

    Scenes 1 to 3 are real paperweight runs on OpenAI's gpt-6-luna. That is not the model the rules were tuned on, so they show how paperweight behaves, not how right it is. Its accuracy still waits on the real human grades.

    Borrowed reviewers

    Scenes 4 and 5 use ICLR 2025's public reviews, where real people scored machine-learning papers. They tested the harness's own maths on real human disagreement, with no AI involved. They say nothing about paperweight itself.

    What changed because of it

    • Papers the AI flip-flops on are now flagged on every report.
    • The better-or-worse verdict now uses a fairer test that keeps false alarms near 5%.
    • The doc for Vinnie now asks for around 40 graded papers.
